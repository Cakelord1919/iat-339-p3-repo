Turning critique into spatial progression

‘Something Lurking’ was a project I developed for IAT 445 teams, a course that requires students to create immersive VR games through a sense of scale. In our early versions, we treated the rooms, puzzles and storyline as separate elements. During playtesting, feedback indicated that the game’s progression was unclear and the storyline was very difficult to follow. I gathered this feedback, shared it with the team as a core issue, and we worked on improvements.

I convened a team meeting in the common learning room at the SFU Library, where I wrote the feedback on the blackboard. I subsequently transferred this to Figma for further refinement, restructuring the narrative into a three-act structure. The first act centers on the protagonist’s sense of security being shattered by a sudden accident whilst repairing an electrical box (ACT 1), followed by an encounter with a monster in the corridor and a frantic escape (ACT 2), and concluding with an open-ended ending where the protagonist hears the monster’s screech whilst inside the escape pod (ACT 3).

After finalizing the narrative, I created 3D models based on the game mechanics proposed by the team. I designed the space station, drainage pipes and chip chambers around three scale ratios [1:1, 1:100, 1:1000] , and incorporated them into a wrist-worn device inspired by the Pip-Boy. As Art Director, I modelled all assets in Blender and developed materials using Substance Designer, ensuring a consistent design language across every space to maintain the space station’s visual coherence.
