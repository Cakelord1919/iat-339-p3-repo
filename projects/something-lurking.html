Turning critique into spatial progression

Something Lurking began as a collaborative VR exploration experience set inside an abandoned space station. The course required scale to shape the experience, but our early build treated its rooms, puzzles, and story as separate ideas. During playtesting, feedback pointed to unclear progression, weak puzzle logic, and an emotional arc that did not build enough tension. I collected those comments and used them as the starting problem: the environment needed to explain what happened while also teaching the player what to do next.

I brought the team together at the SFU library and translated the feedback into whiteboard diagrams, level-progression maps, and Figma concepts. We reorganized the experience into three acts. A calm fuse-repair task established safety and interaction rules; a sudden power cut removed that safety; and the escape-pod sequence ended with a sound behind the player. This sequence let environmental change, rather than combat, create suspense.

I then designed the station around three scale states—1:1, 1:100, and 1:1000—and kept controls on a Pip-Boy-inspired wrist interface. Scale switching connected otherwise separate spaces, while the wrist device held objectives, a flashlight, and scale controls inside the world. I modelled most environments in Blender, developed materials in Substance Designer, and repeated the padded-wall language across scenes to keep the station visually coherent.

Iteration moved continuously between diagrams, models, and the Unity build. Doors, pickup objects, detection zones, lighting, and sound were adjusted so that physical interactions supported the planned sequence. The final demo was selected for an SFU showcase for prospective students. The project clarified my ethos: atmosphere is not decoration; it is a system that can guide attention, establish rules, and turn movement through space into narrative.
