Designing urgency in three days

'The Last of the Stars' is a game I designed for the SFU Summer Summit, a three-day game jam, as part of a team comprising three other members.

We designed a 2D platformer telling the story of a star-shaped alien astronaut trying to escape from a desolate planet. Players must use the mouse to draw temporary platforms that gradually disappear, meaning moving upwards requires careful planning and hand-eye coordination. I was responsible for creating the art assets and designing the levels, ensuring the mechanics were clear and fun without compromising the game’s difficulty.

We spent most of the first day debating two approaches: an ‘ink’ mechanic or a Tetris-style movement system. Once the team had settled on the ink mechanic, I drew inspiration from 'Blade Runner' to establish an art style featuring orange industrial tones, and I drew a climbing route map in Aseprite. My sketches labelled various interactive objects and marked out the routes.

The game loop was then firmly established: players needed to observe, map out their route and then climb. Each dark, distinct platform was clearly visible, helping players to scan the environment and easily determine their next move. During testing, green proved to be a color with high contrast, so we settled on green as the ink color. As the programmers gradually integrated the mechanics, I adjusted the layout based on actual gameplay feedback, rather than rigidly adhering to the sketches.

Although our final version was playable, our project management revealed some issues. Spending the first day discussing mechanics was clearly a waste of time, resulting in a large empty area left in the top section; we didn’t even have time to implement the fan button mechanic.
